# ORIANA — Brand Guidelines

**Document 01 of the Oriana Guideline Suite · Version 2.0 · July 2026**

These guidelines govern every expression of the Oriana brand — the name, the mark, the palette, the type, the imagery, and the standards that keep them coherent. Version 2.0 marks the brand's first deliberate evolution: from a single-service lash identity toward a **beauty house** — warmer, brighter, more human, and open to absolutely anyone. They are written to be used: short enough to actually read, strict enough to keep one artist looking like a brand, and flexible enough to grow from a one-chair HDB studio to whatever Oriana becomes.

---

## 1. The Brand in One Page

**Name:** Oriana (oh-ree-AH-na) — from Latin roots meaning "the rising golden light." In literature, the beloved of Amadís in medieval romance and the poetic name Renaissance madrigalists gave Elizabeth I.

**What we are:** A private beauty studio in Singapore — lashes today, a house of quiet beauty work tomorrow.

**The idea:** The golden hour — the light in which everyone looks like themselves, only more so. Nothing added; the light simply finds what was already there.

**The promise:** Lashes people assume are yours.

**Primary tagline:** *Noticed, not announced.*

**Inclusivity line:** *Beauty, for absolutely anyone.* Oriana photographs, welcomes, and speaks to men and women equally. This is not a campaign; it is the house rule.

**Descriptor lockup:** ORIANA · Studio — deliberately unqualified, so the name never has to change when the services grow.

**Personality in five words:** Warm. Literary. Unhurried. Honest. Bright.

**The one test every brand decision must pass:** *Would this feel at home in golden-hour light — warm, natural, unforced? If it shouts, it fails. If it excludes, it also fails.*

## 2. Brand Architecture — the Beauty House

Oriana is now structured as a **masterbrand house**. The name carries no service in it, which means every future room of the house opens under the same roof without a rebrand. Chesa remains the artist and the face; the brand carries the world (name, light, ritual, standards) and the artist carries the trust (skill, honesty, the documented craft journey). Written expression follows one rule: **Oriana speaks in the first-person plural or the impersonal editorial voice; Chesa speaks as "I" only in artist-voice contexts** (Instagram captions signed –C., the Studio page, WhatsApp conversations). Never blur the two in a single sentence.

| Layer | Name | Status | Notes |

| House descriptor | **Oriana Studio** | Live | The everyday spoken and written name |
| Short form | **Ori** | Live | Captions, watermarks, in-world nickname only |
| Lash line | **Lashes at Oriana** (the menu carries the world-names) | Live | The founding room of the house |
| Brow line | **Oriana Brow** | Reserved | Eyebrow tattooing / brow architecture |

New house lines launch as "Oriana + plain noun" (Brow, Skin, Hair, Spa) — never a second fantasy name; the poetry lives inside each line's menu, not on its door. Mystery is welcome; confusion is not.

## 3. The Wordmark

The logotype is **ORIANA** set in a **thin, high-contrast serif** — hairline strokes against fuller stems, all caps, tracked wide (approximately +14% to +20% of point size). Version 2.0 deliberately thins the mark: lighter weight, higher stroke contrast, more air. The wordmark must read instantly and cleanly at every size; clarity of the mark is a stated brand requirement, not a nicety.

**Primary lockup:** ORIANA over the descriptor **STUDIO** in the quiet sans, letterspaced wider than the wordmark, at roughly one-fifth the wordmark's cap height. The former descriptor "Private Lash Studio" is retired from the lockup; privacy and lashes are told in the copy, not carried in the logo.

**Secondary mark (the glyph):** a low sun above a horizon line, drawn in a single stroke, doubling as an open eye with light rising behind the lash line. Used as watermark, label stamp, and favicon. Unchanged.

**Short-form mark:** "Ori" with the sun-point — for social watermarks and tight spaces only.

Clear space around the wordmark equals the cap height of the O on all sides. Minimum sizes: wordmark 24 px / 8 mm print equivalent; glyph 16 px. Never: stretch, outline, add shadows to the wordmark; set it in another typeface; fill the letterforms with a gradient; place it over busy photography without a scrim. The wordmark may sit **on** a gradient-grain surface, but the letterforms themselves stay solid ink or solid cream.

## 4. Colour — the Gradient-Grain System

Version 2.0 keeps the golden-hour soul and lets more light in. The palette now works on two levels: **solid inks** for structure and text, and a **gradient-grain atmosphere** for surfaces — soft blends of peach, pink, butter gold, eucalyptus green, and powder blue, always finished with a fine film-grain noise so colour feels printed and organic, never like a screensaver.

### Solid inks (structure)

| Role | Name | Hex | Usage share | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary ground | **Cream Hour** | #FAF4EA | ~50% | Brighter than the old Ivory Hour; backgrounds, cards |
| Primary ink | **Umber** | #3B2F26 | ~25% | All body text, wordmark, line work — unchanged |
| Warm accent | **Honey Gold** | #C9963F | ≤5% | Rules, glyph, display numerals — never body text |
| Green accent | **Eucalyptus** | #7E9C7B | ≤10% | The new voice of v2.0: labels, secondary buttons, botanical moments |
| Bright moment | **Sunset Coral** | #E8785A | ≤3% | One per surface at most: a link hover, a single highlighted word |

### Atmosphere (gradient-grain stops)

| Stop | Name | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Peach Veil** | #F6C9A8 |
| 2 | **Petal Pink** | #EFB7B0 |
| 3 | **Butter Gold** | #EBCE8F |
| 4 | **Eucalyptus Mist** | #B7C9AC |
| 5 | **Powder Blue** | #C3D3DC |

Gradient rules: blends are **soft, blurred, and organic** — radial or freeform, never hard linear bands. Every gradient surface carries a grain/noise overlay at 4–8% opacity (SVG turbulence or a film-grain texture). Green appears in **every** multi-stop gradient — it is the signature of Version 2.0. Text sitting on gradient surfaces is always Umber, and gradients stay light enough to pass WCAG AA against it. Gradients are atmosphere, not decoration: hero fields, section transitions, card washes — never inside the wordmark, never behind dense body copy.

Forbidden territory: pink-glitter, black-and-gold luxe, greige minimalism, clinical blue-white, pure white (#FFFFFF), pure black (#000000), and neon of any kind.

## 5. Typography

Two families, two jobs — with the display voice deliberately **thinned** in Version 2.0:

| Role | Style | Reference faces (in order of preference) | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display / headlines / wordmark | Thin high-contrast serif | Cormorant Garamond (Light 300 / Regular 400), Italiana, Playfair Display (Light) | Light weights only at display sizes; sentence case for headlines; all-caps + letterspacing reserved for the wordmark and section labels; a serif italic word mixed into a roman headline is a signature move (one italic word per headline, maximum) |
| Body / UI / prices | Quiet grotesque sans | Inter, Karla, Work Sans | Regular for body; medium for labels; never bold-shouting |

The cursive script (Petit Formal Script) survives in exactly one role: **Chesa's signature and her quoted words** — never headlines, never labels. Hierarchy discipline: one display headline per surface; body at 16–18 px with line height 1.6; prices always in the sans (prices are information, not poetry).

## 6. Photography & Imagery

Version 2.0 moves the camera **closer and more honest**. The canon, in priority order: macro eye and lash-line work where freckles, pores, and skin texture are visible and celebrated; hands and tools mid-craft; window light in the studio; the reveal moment (consent required); the dog (lounge only). Full standards live in Document 06; the three non-negotiables are:

1. **Imperfections are the point.** Freckles, texture, and fine lines stay in the frame and in the file. No skin smoothing, no retouching. An edited lash photo is a lie about the product.
2. **Everyone is castable.** Men and women appear in brand imagery as equals — not as a "men's grooming" sub-campaign, simply as clients of the house.
3. **One grade, forever.** Warm, golden, softly grained — consistent across every surface.

Never: ring-light blue-white clinical shots, heavy filters, stock photography of models we didn't lash, collages, or more than one typeface on an image.

## 7. Materials & Applications

| Application | Standard |
|---|---|
| Aftercare card — "Keep the Light" | A6, Cream Hour stock, Umber ink, gold rule; care instructions styled as a short morning-and-evening rite; The Return booking prompt on the reverse |
| Appointment card | Same stock; date/time in sans, one serif line: *your hour is held* |
| Labels (serum, cleanser retail) | Glyph + ORIANA + product plain-name; no ingredient poetry — clarity sells |
| Linen headrest cover | Sun-point glyph embroidered in gold thread, 20 mm |
| WhatsApp display | Glyph avatar on cream; display name "Oriana Studio" |
| Gradient-grain surfaces | Reserved for hero fields, packaging interiors, story backgrounds — always grained, always with green in the blend |

## 8. Do and Don't — the Guardrails

**Do:** keep at least 40% empty space on every layout; let the photography carry humanity and the type carry authority; show real skin at real magnification; feature men and women; name real numbers (retention weeks, set counts) — proof is a brand asset; write prices plainly; keep the Korean-technique lineage in the craft story (Studio page, education posts) where it earns trust.

**Don't:** put "Korean" — or any single service — in the logo, name, or descriptor; run gradients behind body text or inside the wordmark; use more than one bright-coral moment per surface; retouch skin; discount publicly (the brand offers *The Return* pricing structure, not promotions); use urgency mechanics ("3 slots left!!"); use emoji in editorial surfaces (WhatsApp conversation is exempt — warmth beats purity there); ever show another studio's work.
