# ORIANA — Marketing Guidelines

**Document 03 of the Oriana Guideline Suite · Version 1.0 · July 2026**

This document governs how Oriana acquires, converts, and keeps clients. It translates the brand strategy's funnel into operating rules: which channels do which jobs, how pricing behaves in public, what the referral machine looks like, and what gets measured. The prime directive is unchanged from the strategy: **retention is the brand** — every marketing decision is subordinate to the rebooking rate.

---

## 1. The Funnel, Phase by Phase

Marketing intensity follows the business plan's phase gates. Nothing jumps a gate the practice tracker hasn't confirmed.

| Phase | Business state | Marketing job | Levers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Foundation** (now → certification) | Training, 100 practice sets | Build the audience before the doors open | The documented craft journey; the "100 sets before the golden hour opens" counter; name-story pinned post |
| **1 — Model era** | Practice sets on real models | Convert followers into models; harvest proof | Model call posts; before/afters begin; first testimonials; Google Business Profile goes live |
| **2 — Open** | Real pricing, real clients | Fill the calendar to target utilization | Full menu launch; review engine; referral programme; local SEO; press pitches |
| **3 — Full** | Waitlist forming | Protect margin, deepen retention | Price review; waitlist mechanics; retail attach (Keepsake Kit); consider The Long Light (bridal) |

## 2. Channel Roles

One channel per job; no channel does two jobs.

| Channel | Job | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** | The shop window — discovery and proof | Runs on the four content pillars (see Doc 04). Never the booking rail: every CTA routes to WhatsApp |
| **WhatsApp** | The booking rail — conversion and care | Response target: under 2 hours, 9am–9pm. Every conversation ends with a held hour or a clear next step |
| **Google Business Profile** | Local search capture | Category: eyelash service. Keywords carried in services list and posts ("Korean lash," "natural lash extensions," neighbourhood name). Review target: 25+ at 4.9★ by end of Phase 2 |
| **Lemon8** | Secondary discovery (SG-specific) | Repurposed Instagram content, native captions; no separate production budget |
| **TikTok** | Reserved | Handle held, dormant until Phase 2+ and only if a repeatable format exists |
| **Press / listicles** | Authority (Phase 2) | Pitch the story angle (the golden-hour studio; the 100-set apprenticeship in public), not the discount |

## 3. Pricing in Public

Prices are stated plainly on the website and menu — hiding prices costs more leads than it filters. The brand never discounts publicly and never runs urgency mechanics. Price movements happen at phase gates, announced to existing clients first, framed as the studio maturing, with existing clients grandfathered for one cycle. The model era's reduced pricing is framed as **the model programme** (a defined arrangement with expectations: photos, feedback, punctuality), never as "cheap lashes."

The only public price-adjacent mechanics allowed: **The Return** (infill pricing inside 3 weeks — structurally rewards rebooking), the **referral thank-you** (see §4), and the **Keepsake Kit bundle** at rebooking.

## 4. The Referral Engine

Referrals should carry more than 40% of new clients by mid-Phase 2. The machine has three parts. First, the *aftercare card* physically travels — every client leaves with one, and it is designed to be photographed and asked about. Second, the *referral thank-you*: when a referred client completes her first set, both women receive S$15 off their next Return — framed as a thank-you, delivered privately on WhatsApp, never advertised as a scheme. Third, the *review-to-referral bridge*: the review ask (day 10–14, only after an unprompted compliment) doubles as the moment to mention that most of the calendar comes from friends of clients.

## 5. Local Marketing

The studio serves a radius, not a nation. Neighbourhood name appears in the Instagram bio, Google profile, and website metadata. Phase 2 tactics, in order of expected yield: Google review velocity (the single highest-leverage local asset), partnerships with adjacent non-competing home businesses (nails, brows, hair — reciprocal story mentions, shared aftercare-card racks), and one community anchor (the dog is a legitimate neighbourhood character; walks are marketing). Paid social is **not** used before Phase 2, and then only boosted proof-posts targeted within the radius, capped at a test budget, evaluated on WhatsApp conversations started — never on reach.

## 6. What Gets Measured

Four numbers, reviewed monthly, in this order of importance:

1. **Rebooking rate** (target: 70% by end of Phase 2) — the brand's true health metric
2. **Profile-to-WhatsApp conversion** — are the shop window and the CTA working
3. **Referral share of new clients** (target: >40% by mid-Phase 2)
4. **Google reviews** (target: 25+ at 4.9★ by end of Phase 2)

Explicitly not KPIs: follower count, likes, reach. They are weather, not climate.

## 7. Marketing Time Budget

Brand and marketing work is capped so it never cannibalizes craft hours (8–10 weekly). The sustainable rhythm: content capture happens *inside* existing sets (the fixed photo corner makes this a 90-second overhead per client), one weekly batching session of 60–90 minutes produces the week's posts, and WhatsApp is answered in two daily windows plus ad-hoc. If marketing ever exceeds five hours a week, cut the lowest pillar (The World) first — proof posts are never cut.
