# GYEOL — Brand & Brand Strategy

**A ground-up brand build for Chesa's home-based Korean lash studio in Singapore**

Prepared July 2026 · Companion to *The Lash Build — Business Plan Summary*

---

## How to read this document

Chesa — your business plan already answers *what business you are in* and *how the money works*. This document answers the question that sits underneath every lead you will ever generate: **why would a stranger choose you, remember you, and come back every three weeks?** That is what a brand is. Everything here is built to be executed by one person, on a phased timeline, alongside a day job — nothing in this strategy requires a budget beyond what your Phase 0 investment already contemplates.

The document is organised in the order you should build: foundation (Part I), verbal and visual identity (Part II), experience design (Part III), the content and lead engine (Part IV), and a 90-day activation sequence with measurement (Part V). Open decisions from your plan are resolved or given a recommendation in each relevant section.

# Part I — Brand Foundation

## 1. The one-sentence brand

> **Gyeol is a private Korean lash studio in Singapore where lashes are designed to look like they grew that way — light, healthy, and unmistakably yours.**

Every decision below serves this sentence. If a future choice — a post, a promo, a service, a piece of decor — does not make this sentence more true, don't do it.

## 2. Why the market leaves this space open

Your research and mine converge on the same map of the Singapore lash market. The premium tier (Dreamlash, J.Lashes, Private Room, The Lash Company) charges S$98–198+ and trades on Korean technique, signature methods, and retail locations. The home-based tier (S$25–88) is large, accepted, and almost uniformly branded the same way: English lash-pun names (Lashed by Van, L.ASHED, Lashie SG, Celash, Dear Lash), volume-tier menus (1D–9D), and price-led Instagram feeds. What almost no home studio does is *brand like the premium tier while pricing like the accessible tier's upper end* [1] [2].

Three proof points from the market shape this strategy:

| Market evidence | What it proves | How Gyeol uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **J.Lashes** started home-based, named a signature technique ("Tutu lash" for Asian eyes), and became one of Singapore's most recognised boutiques at S$108+ [1] | A named signature method is the single strongest premiumisation lever for a home studio | Gyeol names its signature set from day one (Section 8) |
| **The Cozy Studio** ("Jess", famous for YY Lashes) is a home studio featured in Her World alongside retail salons [1] | Editorial press is attainable for home studios, and the artist's name carries the trust | Hybrid brand architecture: Gyeol the studio, Chesa the face (Section 4) |
| **3Lash** thrives as an openly pet-friendly home studio [2] | The Cavapoo can be a deliberate differentiator, not a liability | Dog policy becomes a brand feature with a dog-free option (Section 12) |

The Korean-natural trend is validated but **not yet owned by any single home studio brand** — competitors like Serenity Beaute and Bling Blink use the Korean angle as a feature, not an identity [3]. The window is open; it will not stay open.

## 3. Positioning statement

**For** women in Singapore who want lashes that enhance rather than announce themselves, **Gyeol** is the private Korean-style lash studio **that** designs light, wispy, lash-health-first sets people assume are natural — **because** every set is mapped to your eye by an artist trained in the Korean-technique lineage, in a calm one-client-at-a-time home studio, at a price retail salons can't match for the same quality.

The three positioning pillars, in order of importance:

1. **Korean-natural artistry.** The sharpest wedge, exactly as your plan states. It signals light, wispy, lash-health-first work — the opposite of the "spidery Russian volume" cliché — and it is the fastest-growing preference in Singapore. Every visual, caption, and service name reinforces it.
2. **Private, one-at-a-time care.** The home studio is reframed from a cost advantage into an experience advantage: no salon floor, no upselling scripts, no strangers, one artist whose full attention is on your eyes for two hours. Market pain points — pushy retail sales tactics and inconsistent results across technicians — become Gyeol's foil [2].
3. **Lash health as non-negotiable.** Retention is the entire reputation, as your plan says. Publicly committing to natural-lash health (refusing over-heavy sets, honest infill assessments, safe peptide aftercare retail only) converts an operational discipline into a trust story.

**Brand promise:** *Lashes people assume are yours.* This stays verbatim from the plan — it is genuinely excellent. It is the headline of the Instagram bio, the website hero, and the answer when anyone asks what makes Gyeol different.

**Pricing stance:** mid-premium from day one of real pricing (Phase 2: classic S$68–88), never competing on cheap. The brand's job during Phases 0–1 is to *accumulate the evidence* that makes those prices feel obvious.

## 4. Brand architecture: the studio and the artist

Your plan flags the tension correctly: Singapore home studios run on the artist's personal name because clients are entering a stranger's flat, yet a personal-name brand is hard to scale or sell. The resolution is a **two-layer architecture** used by the strongest home studios:

| Layer | Name | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **The brand** | Gyeol | The studio, the promise, the visual identity, the thing that can one day hold a second artist, a training arm, or a retail line |
| **The face** | Chesa | The trust signal — face in Stories, name in captions ("mapped and set by Chesa"), the person clients form a relationship with |

In practice: the Instagram handle and all assets belong to Gyeol; Chesa appears constantly *within* them. Clients say "I go to Chesa at Gyeol." Trust accrues to the person; equity accrues to the brand. When teaching or a second chair arrives in Phase 3, the brand can hold it without a rename.

## 5. The audience, made specific

"Women in Singapore who want lashes" is not an audience; it is a census. Gyeol's early growth comes from three concrete segments, prioritised by lifetime value and reachability:

| Segment | Who she is | What she wants | Why Gyeol wins her |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Natural-First Professional** (primary) | 25–40, works in town, wears little eye makeup, has had lashes before and hated looking "done" — or has been lash-curious but scared of the spidery look | To look rested and polished on Zoom and in person, without anyone clocking that she's had work done | The entire brand promise is engineered for her; she rebooks like clockwork and refers colleagues |
| **The K-Beauty Native** (secondary) | 20–35, follows Korean beauty trends on Instagram/Lemon8/TikTok, already asks salons for "Korean style" | The authentic version of the look she saves on Pinterest, from someone who actually understands it | Korean-language name, Korea Eyelash Association-lineage certification story, correct terminology and styling vocabulary |
| **The Salon Refugee** (tertiary) | Any age; burned by inconsistent results, pushy packages, or lash damage at retail salons — a complaint that appears verbatim in Singapore expat groups [2] | Consistency, honesty, and her natural lashes intact | One artist every time, health-first policies, no packages ever pushed |

Geography matters for a home studio: the realistic catchment is roughly a 25–30 minute radius. Local targeting (neighbourhood hashtags, Google Business Profile, nearby-office lunchtime positioning later on) beats island-wide reach in every phase before a waitlist exists.

## 6. Competitive frame

| | Retail premium (Dreamlash, J.Lashes) | Typical home studio | **Gyeol** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price (classic set) | S$98–168 | S$25–60 | S$68–88 at real pricing |
| Styling philosophy | Korean/signature methods | Volume-tier menus (1D–9D) | Korean-natural, mapped per eye, named signature sets |
| Experience | Retail floor, multiple technicians | Functional, variable | Private, one artist, ritualised (Part III) |
| Brand | Professional, established | Lash-pun names, price-led feeds | Editorial, Korean-minimal, story-led |
| Trust mechanism | Awards, longevity | Price and proximity | Chesa's visible craft journey + retention proof |

The strategic posture in one line: **premium-tier brand codes at upper-home-studio prices** — clients feel they've discovered a secret, which is precisely the psychology that fuels referrals.

## 7. Name decision and verification

**Recommendation: commit to Gyeol (결).** The verification research supports the plan's lean. No Singapore lash or beauty-service business operates under the name; the nearest uses are a brow studio in Seoul, a hair studio in Busan, and online K-beauty skincare brands — different categories, different geographies [4]. The word's meaning is a gift to brand storytelling:

> 결 (gyeol) — the grain or texture of a thing; the natural direction in which a strand lies. It is used of wood, of water, of hair — of anything whose beauty comes from following its own nature rather than fighting it [5].

That is not just a pretty etymology; it is the styling philosophy in a single syllable. Working *with* the natural lash — its direction, its health, its cycle — is literally what Korean-natural technique does and what the brand promise claims.

Two practical accommodations, because a Korean word unfamiliar to most Singaporeans needs scaffolding:

1. **Always use the descriptor lockup** in public-facing contexts: **GYEOL · Korean Lash Studio**. The descriptor does the category work the name alone cannot, and it carries the "Korean lash Singapore" search phrase clients actually type.
2. **Teach the pronunciation and meaning proactively** — a pinned Instagram post and a line on the website ("*gyeol* — the natural grain of a strand"). A name people learn to say is a name people remember; the mild friction becomes a conversation starter and a brand-story delivery vehicle.

**Lock it this week, in this order:** (1) ACRA business name search and reservation, (2) Instagram handle — target @gyeol.lash, fallbacks @gyeolstudio.sg or @gyeol.sg, (3) domain — gyeol.sg preferred with gyeollash.com as fallback, (4) TikTok and Lemon8 handles to match even if unused for now. Do all four before announcing anything anywhere.

# Part II — Verbal & Visual Identity

## 8. The signature set: naming the craft

The clearest lesson from Singapore's success stories is that a **named signature technique** is what converts skill into pricing power — J.Lashes has "Tutu", The Cozy Studio has "YY" [1]. Gyeol should launch real pricing (Phase 2) with a named hero set:

> **The Gyeol Set** — the studio's signature Korean-natural design: ultra-fine extensions mapped to the client's eye shape and natural lash direction, built for softness, lash health, and three-week retention.

Naming the signature after the studio itself is deliberate: every mention of the hero product advertises the brand, and it sidesteps inventing a second brand name to protect. The supporting menu then reads as a coherent family rather than a commodity price list:

| Menu name | Underlying service | Phase 2 price |
|---|---|---|
| The Gyeol Set | Signature Korean-natural classic set | S$78–88 |
| Gyeol Volume | Korean natural-volume (once certified) | S$130–160 |
| The Lift | Korean lash lift + tint | S$58–68 |
| The Refresh | Infill within 3 weeks | S$45–55 |
| Aftercare | Peptide serum + branded cleanser retail | retail margin |

Note what is absent: no 1D–9D jargon, no "mega volume", no cluster lashes. The menu itself is a positioning document. (Per the plan's correction: falsies never appear on the menu — they are practice, not product; and the aftercare serum is a retailed peptide product, never an in-house formulation, keeping Gyeol clear of HSA-regulated territory.)

## 9. Brand story

The story clients and journalists will retell, in its canonical form — use it on the website About page, the Instagram pinned post, and every press pitch:

> Gyeol (결) is the Korean word for the grain of a thing — the natural direction a strand wants to lie. It's how Chesa approaches every set of lashes: not as an addition, but as a continuation of what's already there. Trained in the Korean technique lineage, she opened Gyeol as a private one-client-at-a-time studio because the best lash work isn't done on a salon floor — it's done in quiet, with time, for eyes she'll know for years, not appointments she'll never see again. The goal of every set is the compliment that matters most: *"wait, those are extensions?"*

Three story rules keep it honest across phases. During Phase 0–1, the story is told in progress form — "training in the Korean lineage", "building toward opening" — which is itself compelling content (Section 15). The certification lineage (Korea Eyelash Association pathway via The Lash Academy) is named specifically once earned, because specificity is what separates a credential from a claim. And Chesa's documented craft journey — the hundred sets, the timed improvement, the retention data from the tracker — becomes public proof that most lash artists never think to show.

## 10. Voice and verbal identity

Gyeol speaks like **a knowledgeable friend with a good eye** — warm, precise, unhurried, never salesy. The voice has three registers, and knowing which register a given touchpoint uses prevents the tonal drift that makes small brands feel inconsistent:

| Register | Where | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Editorial** | Website, menu, pinned posts, press | "Every set begins with a map of your eye — its shape, its lash direction, what your natural lashes can healthily carry." |
| **Companion** | Captions, Stories, WhatsApp | "Three weeks on and still this full — this is why we don't overload your natural lashes." |
| **Care** | Aftercare cards, reminders, policies | "Your lashes shed on their own cycle — an infill every 2–3 weeks keeps the set seamless. Reply here and I'll find you a slot." |

Vocabulary discipline does quiet work: say *set, map, design, natural lash health, retention, infill*; never say *falsies, spidery, mega, cheap, promo price*. Discounts are framed as access ("model rates while I complete my certification journey — openly, and only until the standard is met"), never as desperation. WhatsApp — the booking channel Singapore expects [2] — uses the Companion register with the Care register's reliability: confirmations, aftercare follow-ups at 48 hours, and rebooking nudges all have a consistent, unmistakably Gyeol sound.

**Tagline system** (use one primary, rotate the others as campaign lines):

- Primary: **Lashes people assume are yours.**
- "The natural grain of beautiful." (name-meaning line)
- "Designed in quiet. Worn everywhere." (private-studio line)
- "Korean-natural, one eye at a time." (category line)

## 11. Visual identity direction

The visual brief in one phrase: **Korean-minimal editorial** — the restraint of Korean beauty branding (think Tamburins, Nonfiction) applied to lash artistry, which instantly separates Gyeol from both the pink-glitter home-studio cliché and the black-and-gold "luxury lash" cliché [6].

| Element | Direction | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Logotype** | GYEOL in a refined serif or high-contrast sans, wide letterspacing; 결 in hangul as a small secondary mark; descriptor "Korean Lash Studio" in a light sans beneath | The hangul character is an ownable visual asset no competitor can copy honestly |
| **Symbol** | A single flowing "grain" stroke — one curved line suggesting both a lash and the grain of wood/water that 결 denotes | One-stroke marks scale from watermark to signage to aftercare card |
| **Palette** | Warm ivory (#F7F3EE) base, soft greige (#B8AEA2), deep ink-brown (#2B2320) for type, one accent of muted celadon or dusty clay used sparingly | Calm, editorial, photographs well against skin tones; deliberately not pink, not black-and-gold |
| **Typography** | Serif for headlines (editorial authority), clean geometric sans for body and prices | Mirrors K-beauty editorial codes |
| **Photography** | Natural light, close-crop eye shots on clean skin, negative space, before/afters in a fixed template with consistent angle and lighting; Chesa's hands and tools as recurring motifs | Consistency is the entire game on Instagram — a fixed capture setup (same spot, same ring light, same angle) makes an amateur feed look professional overnight [7] |
| **Materials** | Aftercare card, appointment card, serum/cleanser labels, a simple linen headrest cover with the grain-stroke mark | The branded aftercare card is simultaneously a retention tool and a take-home ad [8] |

A practical note on execution order, keeping the early build unembellished: in Phase 0, only four assets are needed — logotype lockup, palette, one Instagram template set (before/after frame, story cover, price/menu card), and the aftercare card. Everything else (packaging, signage-equivalents, website polish) waits until the model era proves the look against real content.

# Part III — The Brand Experience

## 12. The studio ritual

For a home studio, the *experience is the brand* in its most literal form — clients are lying down with their eyes closed in your flat for two hours. Design the sequence once, run it identically every time; consistency is what converts a nice visit into a ritual clients narrate to friends [8]:

1. **Before arrival** — WhatsApp confirmation with a photo of the building entrance and exact walking directions, what to avoid beforehand (mascara, oils, caffeine right before), and the dog policy note. Zero ambiguity; entering a stranger's home requires over-communication.
2. **Arrival** — a consistent scent (one diffuser, one scent, forever — scent is the strongest memory anchor), slippers, water or barley tea, phone basket offered but never pushed.
3. **The mapping conversation** — five unhurried minutes: eye shape, lash health assessment, lifestyle, the look she's imagining. This is where Gyeol visibly differs from every walk-in salon; photograph the lash map itself (with permission) — it is outstanding content.
4. **The set** — calm playlist, warm blanket, no phone calls taken, a mid-set check-in whisper.
5. **The reveal** — hand mirror moment, then the photo in the fixed capture setup. Ask permission for posting with a simple standing consent note.
6. **The send-off** — aftercare card + mini cleanser, verbal 3-line aftercare summary, and **the rebooking ask, every single time**: "Your infill window is the week of the 14th — shall I hold Tuesday evening or Saturday morning?" The plan's 70% rebooking target is won or lost in this sentence; offering two concrete slots roughly doubles uptake versus "message me when you're ready."
7. **48 hours later** — a WhatsApp check-in: "How are the lashes settling? Any irritation at all, tell me immediately." This message does triple duty: safety net, care signal, and the moment to request a Google review once the studio is public.

**The Cavapoo decision (open decision #3): make Gyeol deliberately dog-friendly, with a guaranteed dog-free option.** The market proof exists — 3Lash operates successfully as an openly pet-friendly studio [2] — and a Cavapoo is a genuinely differentiating, deeply Instagram-friendly brand character for a *private home* studio in a way it never could be for a retail salon. The execution rules from your plan stand: it must be advertised, never a surprise; the lash room itself stays dog-free during treatments (hygiene and adhesive safety); the booking flow includes a "dog-free visit" toggle for allergic or anxious clients; and the puppy gets a name-credit in the brand world (studio greeter, occasional Stories star). Decide the policy language before the first paying client, exactly as the plan requires.

## 13. Trust infrastructure

Because clients are entering a home, trust must be *engineered*, not hoped for. The checklist: HDB Home-Based Business Scheme registration (mentioned quietly on the website — it signals legitimacy), treatment liability insurance (worth stating: "fully insured"), a visible hygiene protocol (sealed single-use items opened in front of the client, sterilisation shown in Stories), a patch-test offer for first-timers, and an honest-refusal policy — publicly stating that Gyeol will decline to apply a heavier set than the client's natural lashes can carry. That last policy costs a few bookings and builds the reputation that fills the waitlist.

# Part IV — The Content & Lead Engine

## 14. Channel strategy

| Channel | Role | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** | Primary brand home and lead source; where Singapore finds lash artists [2] | 3–4 feed posts/week, daily Stories once operating |
| **WhatsApp** | Booking and retention rail; every CTA everywhere points here | Always-on, templated flows |
| **Lemon8** | Growing SG beauty-discovery platform; long-form "my lash journey" posts perform well [2] | 1–2/week, repurposed |
| **TikTok** | Process video reach (mapping, timelapses); secondary until capacity allows | Repurpose Reels only |
| **Google Business Profile** | "Lash extensions near me" capture + review engine | Set up at first paid client; reviews requested at 48-hour check-in |
| **Press/listicles** | Her World, Daily Vanity, TheSmartLocal home-based roundups — proven attainable for home studios [1] [2] | Pitch in Phase 2 with press kit |

Everything funnels to one link-in-bio page: menu, prices, location area (neighbourhood, not address), dog policy, WhatsApp button.

## 15. Content pillars

Four pillars, mapped to what each is *for* — and note that your plan's insight holds: creative direction is the unfair advantage, because almost no lash artist treats content as a designed system [7].

| Pillar | Share | Content | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Work** | 40% | Before/afters in the fixed template, healed-set check-ins at 3 weeks ("still this full"), mapping diagrams, texture close-ups | Proof. Retention shots are the killer differentiator — anyone can post fresh sets; almost nobody proves week-three |
| **The Craft Journey** | 25% | Certification milestones, practice-set counter ("set 34/100"), timed-set improvements, honest lessons | Builds the audience *before* there is a business; turns Phase 0–1 into a public story people root for |
| **Lash Intelligence** | 20% | Korean vs Russian styling explained, why over-heavy sets damage lashes, aftercare myths, "what your lash artist wishes you knew" | Authority + saves/shares; feeds the algorithm and pre-sells the health-first positioning |
| **The Studio World** | 15% | The ritual details, the scent, the tea, the Cavapoo, Chesa herself, name-meaning storytelling | Emotional connection; the reason clients feel they know you before they arrive |

**Start the account from set one of practice**, exactly as the plan says. The Craft Journey pillar means Phase 0 and the model era are not a content desert — they are the origin story, told live. The "100 sets" counter is a narrative device with a built-in finale: the day the counter completes is the day real pricing launches, to an audience that watched the standard being earned.

## 16. The lead funnel, phase by phase

**Phase 0–1 (audience before revenue).** Leads are practice models: recruited via personal network, office colleagues, and a simple "become a lash model" Story template (model rates S$30–45, openly framed). Every model is asked for two things besides the fee: photo consent and a referral. Target: every week ends with next week's practice slots full.

**Phase 2 (real pricing, 8–12 clients/week).** The engine is referral + retention, assisted by content: (a) the rebooking ritual drives the 70% metric that *is* the business; (b) a simple referral mechanic — "bring a friend to her first Gyeol Set, you both get S$15 off your next infill" — priced against the S$5–15 product cost, making a referred client nearly pure margin; (c) Google reviews accumulate from the 48-hour check-in flow; (d) one local micro-influencer or well-followed colleague per month receives a complimentary set in exchange for honest Stories [7]. No paid ads yet — at 8–12 weekly slots, organic + referral fills capacity, and scarcity itself ("two slots left this week") becomes content.

**Phase 3 (waitlist and pricing power).** When demand exceeds ~25 slots/week: raise prices before adding hours; introduce the waitlist publicly (waitlists are the strongest social proof in service businesses); pitch the press kit (home-studio listicles and "Korean lash" trend pieces actively seek exactly this story [1] [2]); and only then consider small geo-targeted Meta ads for the highest-margin service — with the brand assets by then compounding, ads amplify rather than substitute.

## 17. Measurement

The tracker already measures the business (rebooking rate, set time, revenue, the quit math). The brand needs only four numbers alongside it, checked monthly: **profile-to-WhatsApp conversion** (taps on the booking link ÷ profile visits), **saves + shares per post** (the algorithm's trust signals, and a proxy for authority content working), **referral share of new clients** (target: >40% by mid-Phase 2), and **Google review count and average** (target: 25+ reviews at 4.9 by end of Phase 2). Vanity metrics — follower count above all — are explicitly not KPIs; a 900-follower account of nearby women converts better than 9,000 scattered admirers.

# Part V — Activation: The First 90 Days

Sequenced against the plan's calendar (travel until 25 July; certification start early August), and kept deliberately light during weeks that compete with course hours:

| Weeks | Brand workstream | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **1–2** (late July) | Lock the name: ACRA search + reservation, @gyeol.lash (or fallback), gyeol.sg, matching TikTok/Lemon8 handles. Draft dog-policy language. | Name secured on all four fronts |
| **3–4** (early Aug) | Minimum identity kit: logotype lockup + hangul mark, palette, IG template set (before/after frame, story covers, menu card), aftercare card design. Set up the fixed photo corner (spot, light, angle). | Identity v1, capture setup |
| **5–6** | Instagram launch: bio with promise + descriptor + WhatsApp link; pinned name-story post; first Craft Journey posts (certification begins). Lemon8 mirror account. | Account live before first practice set |
| **7–10** | Content rhythm: 3 posts/week from certification + mannequin practice; practice-set counter begins; model-recruitment Story template ready for when human practice starts. | Audience growing alongside skill |
| **11–13** | Model era prep: booking flow in WhatsApp (confirmation, directions, 48-hour check-in templates), consent note, ritual checklist, dog policy published. First model sets photographed in the fixed setup. | Machine ready; every model documented |

Beyond day 90, the cadence is set by the plan's own gates: identity v2 and the link-in-bio menu page ship with Phase 2 real pricing; the press kit and referral mechanic ship once the rebooking rate holds above 60%; nothing brand-related ever jumps a phase gate the tracker hasn't confirmed.

## A closing note from your co-founder

Chesa — the deepest asset in this whole strategy isn't the name or the palette. It's that you are building the skill and the brand *simultaneously and publicly*, which is something the market's incumbents can never replicate: they can't re-run their origin story. The hundred practice sets your plan demands are also a hundred pieces of content, a hundred referral seeds, and a hundred proofs of the standard you're setting. Protect the 8–10 weekly craft hours above every brand task — a beautiful brand wrapped around mediocre retention dies in this market, while boring branding wrapped around three-week retention merely grows slowly. This strategy exists so you never have to choose: the brand is designed to be built *by* the practice, not alongside it.
