# VESPER — Brand & Brand Strategy (v2)

**A literary, placeless brand for Chesa's private lash studio in Singapore**

Prepared July 2026 · Supersedes the Gyeol strategy · Companion to *The Lash Build — Business Plan Summary*

---

## What changed, and why this version exists

Chesa — you gave three new constraints after the first strategy: the name must read as **literary first** (like Aesop — origin permitted, but never the first thing that comes to mind), the naming territory is **mythological**, and the Korean angle must be **softened and placed under a theme** — the Tomorrowland move, where the world is the brand and the mechanics live inside it. This version rebuilds the naming, the positioning, and the brand world around those constraints. Everything that still held from v1 — the business logic, the audience, the ritual design, the content engine, the phase gates — is carried forward and re-dressed in the new world, so you are not losing work, only re-skinning it around a better spine.

---

# Part I — The Name

## 1. Recommendation: **Vesper**

> **Vesper** (Latin) — the evening star. In Greek myth, *Hesperos*; in Roman poetry, *Vesper* — the planet Venus appearing at dusk, the first light you notice in the sky without quite knowing why you looked up. From the same root: *vespers*, the quiet evening rite [1].

The case, point by point against your brief:

**It reads as literary, not geographic.** Vesper carries no country. Its associations are Roman poetry, evensong, and — for a certain audience — Vesper Lynd, the most quietly magnetic woman in the Bond canon. Like Aesop, it has an origin (Latin) that sits far behind the *feel* (literary, dusky, composed). Nobody's first thought is "Italian brand."

**The myth is exactly your promise.** The evening star is not the brightest object in the sky by force — it is the one your eye goes to first, unbidden. That is the entire brand promise (*lashes people assume are yours*) restated as a myth: **noticed, not announced.** Venus-as-evening-star also happens to be the beauty goddess in her subtlest appearance — Venus without the fanfare. It is difficult to imagine a more precise mythological fit for natural-first lash artistry.

**It sounds and sets beautifully.** Two syllables, six letters, soft V and open E — the same compact, wordmark-friendly shape as Aesop. Easy for anyone in Singapore to say on first read, with just enough unfamiliarity-as-a-name to create the intrigue you asked for ("Vesper... like the evening star" is a one-line story every client can retell).

**The lane is clear where it matters.** Verification found **no Singapore beauty or lash business named Vesper** [2]. The only lash-adjacent uses are two small independent artists in Portland and Denver — different market, no Singapore registration, no meaningful digital collision [3]. By contrast, the strongest alternatives failed: *Selene* is a registered lash boutique in Hougang (direct competitor — this alone shows how right you are to verify before falling in love) [4], *Nyx* and *Eos* are blocked by global cosmetics brands, *Ondine* collides with Little Ondine makeup sold on Shopee SG, and *Fable* is both crowded in Singapore and the too-obvious Aesop imitation.

**Lock list, this week, in order:** ACRA name search and reservation ("Vesper Studio" or "Vesper Beauty" as the registered entity, trading as Vesper); Instagram — target **@vesper.studio.sg** or **@vespersg**, with @vesperlash.sg as a functional fallback; domain — **vesper.sg** preferred, vesperstudio.sg fallback; matching TikTok and Lemon8 handles even if dormant. As with v1: all four before announcing anything.

### Runners-up, kept on file

If Vesper fails at the ACRA or handle stage, the vetted bench in order of preference: **Psyche** (the Eros and Psyche story is *the* myth of beauty and the gaze, and "sy-kee" has lovely intrigue — held back only because a Singapore counselling practice shares the word and the mental-health reading can intrude), and **Halcyon** (the kingfisher myth of calm seas; thematically gorgeous for a sanctuary-studio but drifting toward spa-generic). Blocked outright: Selene, Nyx, Eos, Ondine, Sable (Royal Sable is a lash-tray product brand sold in Singapore [5]), Noctua, Fable.

# Part II — The World

## 2. The theme: *The Evening Hour*

Tomorrowland's trick is that nobody buys a ticket to "an EDM festival in Boom, Belgium" — they buy entry to a world with its own name, mythology, vocabulary, and door. Applied to a one-chair studio, the same trick works at miniature scale: clients don't book "a lash appointment at a home studio in Singapore" — they step into **the evening hour**: a small, private, out-of-time ritual built around the myth of the evening star. The theme does four jobs at once — it makes a home studio feel like a destination, it gives every service and touchpoint a shared language, it demotes technique (Korean or otherwise) from headline to craft detail, and it produces an endless well of content that isn't "another before/after."

The one-sentence brand, restated for the new world:

> **Vesper is a private lash studio where beauty works the way the evening star does — the first thing people notice, and the last thing they can explain.**

## 3. Positioning, with the Korean angle softened

**For** women in Singapore who want lashes that enhance rather than announce themselves, **Vesper** is the private one-client-at-a-time lash studio **that** designs featherlight, lash-health-first sets people assume are natural — **because** every set is mapped to the individual eye with an obsessive, quietly rigorous craft standard, in a calm studio that feels like the hour just after sunset.

The Korean technique now lives exactly where Aesop keeps its plant science: **in the craft story, not on the sign.** In practice this means the certification lineage appears in the About page and in educational content ("trained in the Korean-technique tradition of ultra-fine, lash-health-first work"), the phrase "Korean-natural" survives as a *style descriptor* clients can search for ("our signature sets draw on Korean-natural styling"), and SEO-facing surfaces (Google Business Profile, link-in-bio, hashtags) keep the keywords — because "Korean lash Singapore" remains what the K-Beauty Native segment types into search. What disappears is Korea as identity: no Korean name, no hangul mark, no "Korean Lash Studio" descriptor lockup. The descriptor becomes:

> **VESPER · Private Lash Studio**

The three positioning pillars, reweighted: (1) **the world** — the evening-hour ritual, private and unhurried, the anti-salon; (2) **the craft** — mapped-to-your-eye, featherlight, health-first artistry, with the Korean lineage as supporting evidence; (3) **the proof** — retention shown publicly at week three, honest-refusal policies, the documented craft journey. Audience segments, competitive frame, and pricing stance carry over from v1 unchanged: the same three segments (Natural-First Professional, K-Beauty Native, Salon Refugee), the same posture of *premium-tier brand codes at upper-home-studio prices*, the same mid-premium Phase 2 pricing.

## 4. The service constellation

In a themed world, the menu is world-building's front line. Services take names from the arc of an evening — each one plainly translated so no client is ever confused about what she's buying:

| World name | Plain descriptor | Underlying service | Phase 2 price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First Light** | The signature set | Featherlight classic extensions, mapped per eye (Korean-natural styling) | S$78–88 |
| **Gloaming** | The volume set | Soft natural-volume, once certified | S$130–160 |
| **The Rise** | Lash lift + tint | Keratin lift with tint | S$58–68 |
| **The Return** | Infill within 3 weeks | Signature infill | S$45–55 |
| **Nightfall Kit** | Aftercare at home | Peptide serum + branded cleanser retail | retail margin |

"First Light" is the named hero set — the strategic equivalent of J.Lashes' "Tutu" [6] — and its name *is* the myth: the first light you notice at dusk. The naming system is deliberately expandable (a future bridal package is "The Golden Hour"; a future training arm is "The Observatory") without ever leaving the world. The v1 menu rules stand: no 1D–9D jargon, no falsies on the menu, retailed peptide serum only — never in-house formulation.

## 5. Brand story — canonical version

> There is a moment just after sunset when one light appears before all the others. The Romans called it Vesper — the evening star. It isn't the brightest thing in the sky. It's simply the first thing you notice, and you couldn't say why.
>
> Vesper is a private lash studio built on that idea. One client at a time, in quiet, Chesa maps each set to the eye it belongs to — featherlight work in the lash-health-first tradition she trained in, designed to be mistaken for what nature gave you. No salon floor, no packages, no fanfare. Just the hour, the craft, and the compliment that matters most: *"wait — those are yours?"*

The story rules from v1 carry over: told in progress-form during Phases 0–1 ("building toward opening"), the certification lineage named specifically once earned, the tracker's craft data (set counts, timings, retention) made public as proof.

## 6. Voice and taglines

The voice keeps v1's three registers (Editorial / Companion / Care) with the temperature shifted slightly: **less K-beauty freshness, more dusk-lit composure** — think Aesop's copywriting warmth crossed with a trusted friend on WhatsApp. Vocabulary gains the world's words (*the hour, first light, the return, settle, dusk*) and keeps the craft words (*map, set, natural lash health, retention*); the banned list is unchanged (*falsies, spidery, mega, promo, cheap*).

Tagline system — primary first, others rotate: **"Noticed, not announced."** · "Lashes people assume are yours." (carried over — still the promise) · "The first thing they see. The last thing they'd guess." · "An hour outside the day."

## 7. Visual identity direction

The brief shifts from *Korean-minimal* to **dusk-lit editorial** — Aesop's restraint with the evening hour's palette:

| Element | Direction | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Logotype** | VESPER in a literary serif with wide letterspacing; a small five-pointed star glyph (✦) as the secondary mark, usable as a text ornament | The star glyph is ownable, types anywhere (captions, WhatsApp), and scales from watermark to label |
| **Symbol** | A single star-point above a horizon line — one stroke, evoking both the evening star and a closed eye with one lash | One-stroke marks survive every size; the closed-eye double read rewards a second look |
| **Palette** | Deep dusk-blue ink (#1E2233) and warm ivory (#F5F1EA) as the primary pair; muted apricot-rose (#D9A48F) as the "last light" accent; soft slate (#8B90A0) supporting | Placeless, editorial, photographs beautifully against skin; deliberately not pink-glitter, not black-and-gold, and no longer greige-K-minimal |
| **Typography** | High-contrast literary serif for headlines; quiet grotesque sans for body and prices | The Aesop code: bookish authority, zero decoration |
| **Photography** | Natural light shifted golden-hour warm; close-crop eye work on clean skin; before/afters in one fixed template; recurring motifs of hands, tools, and the studio at dusk | The fixed capture setup rule from v1 stands — consistency is what makes one artist look like a brand [7] |
| **Materials** | Aftercare card ("Until the Return" — care instructions styled as a short evening rite), appointment card, serum/cleanser labels, linen headrest cover with the star glyph | The aftercare card remains a retention tool and take-home ad [8] |

Execution order stays deliberately unembellished for the early build: Phase 0 needs only the logotype lockup, palette, one Instagram template set, and the aftercare card. Everything else waits for real content in the model era.

# Part III — Experience, Engine, and Activation (carried forward, re-dressed)

## 8. The studio ritual → *the evening hour*

The seven-step ritual from v1 transfers intact — confirmation with photo directions, the single signature scent, the mapping conversation, the quiet set, the reveal, the send-off with the rebooking ask ("your Return window is the week of the 14th — Tuesday evening or Saturday morning?"), and the 48-hour check-in. The theme upgrades it from service-sequence to *world-entry*: lighting warm and low like dusk regardless of the clock, the phone basket becomes "leaving the day at the door," and the reveal mirror gets one engraved line — *noticed, not announced*. **The Cavapoo recommendation stands unchanged**: deliberately dog-friendly as an advertised brand feature with a guaranteed dog-free option and a dog-free lash room during treatments — and in this world the puppy has a natural role (the studio's "resident evening companion") [9]. Trust infrastructure is identical to v1: HDB registration noted quietly, insurance stated, visible hygiene protocol, patch tests, and the honest-refusal policy.

## 9. The content and lead engine

Channels, cadence, funnel phases, referral mechanics, and measurement all carry over from v1 unchanged — Instagram primary, WhatsApp as the booking rail, Lemon8 and Google Business Profile supporting, press pitches in Phase 2 [6] [10]. What changes is the flavour of the four pillars:

| Pillar | Share | Re-themed content |
|---|---|---|
| **The Work** | 40% | Before/afters in the fixed template; week-three "still holding" retention posts recaptioned as *"three weeks past first light"* — proof remains the killer differentiator |
| **The Craft Journey** | 25% | The 100-set counter becomes *"100 sets before opening night"* — a countdown with a built-in finale: the counter completing is the day real pricing launches |
| **Lash Intelligence** | 20% | Health-first education, styling explainers (Korean-natural as a style among styles, explained by someone trained in it), aftercare myths |
| **The World** | 15% | The dusk palette, the scent, the ritual details, the dog, Chesa herself, and the evening-star mythology told in fragments — this pillar is where the Tomorrowland move pays daily rent |

## 10. Activation — the first 90 days, resequenced

The calendar logic from v1 holds (travel until 25 July; certification from early August; brand work sized around 8–10 weekly craft hours). Only the deliverables change: weeks 1–2 lock the Vesper name across ACRA, handles, and domain and finalise the dog-policy language; weeks 3–4 produce the minimum identity kit in the dusk palette plus the fixed photo corner; weeks 5–6 launch Instagram with the pinned evening-star story post and the "100 sets before opening night" counter announcement; weeks 7–10 run the certification-era content rhythm; weeks 11–13 ready the model-era machine (WhatsApp flows, consent note, ritual checklist, first documented model sets). Beyond day 90, nothing brand-related jumps a phase gate the tracker hasn't confirmed — unchanged from v1.

## 11. Measurement

Identical to v1, because the numbers were never the problem: profile-to-WhatsApp conversion, saves and shares per post, referral share of new clients (>40% by mid-Phase 2), and Google reviews (25+ at 4.9 by end of Phase 2). Follower count remains explicitly not a KPI.

## A closing note from your co-founder

The instinct you had — that the first name was carrying the country when it should have been carrying the *feeling* — is the same instinct Aesop's founders had, and it is the right one for a brand you might one day scale past your own two hands. What I'd protect fiercely now is the discipline underneath the prettiness: Vesper only works if the week-three retention posts keep appearing, because a mythology wrapped around mediocre craft is just a costume. The world gives people a reason to look; the craft gives them a reason to stay. Build both in the order the plan already demands, and the name will feel inevitable within a year.
