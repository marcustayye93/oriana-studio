# ORIANA — Brand & Brand Strategy (v3)

**A soft, literary, placeless brand for Chesa's private lash studio in Singapore**

Prepared July 2026 · Supersedes the Vesper strategy (v2) and the Gyeol strategy (v1) · Companion to *The Lash Build — Business Plan Summary*

---

## What changed in this version

You refined the naming brief one more turn: the name must **sound soft**, **roll off the tongue**, and carry its emphasis on an **O**. Vesper failed that test on sound — the V attack and the hard P stop make it consonant-forward, more Bond-girl than beauty ritual. So the naming territory was regenerated against a strict phonetic specification: a leading or emphasized O, only soft consonants (the liquids and nasals — l, r, m, n — plus soft s), open vowels throughout, and no hard stops carrying the word. Every candidate was then re-verified against the Singapore market. The winning name changes; the world built in v2 bends gracefully to fit it, and everything operational (ritual, content engine, funnel, phase gates, activation) carries forward once more.

# Part I — The Name

## 1. Recommendation: **Oriana**

> **Oriana** — oh-ree-AH-na. From Latin *oriens*, "rising" (as the sun rises — the same root as *orient* and *aurora*) and *aurum*, "gold" [1]. In literature: the beloved of the knight Amadís in the great chivalric romance *Amadís de Gaula*, and the poetic name England's madrigal composers gave Queen Elizabeth I — *The Triumphs of Oriana* (1601) is an entire songbook written in praise of her [2].

Against your brief, point by point:

**It is soft, and it rolls.** Say it aloud: *oh-ree-AH-na*. Four open syllables, nothing but liquids and vowels — there isn't a single hard consonant in the word. The O leads, the stress lands on the warm open AH, and the name ends on a soft exhale. Phonetically it is the exact opposite of Vesper: where Vesper strikes, Oriana flows. Of every name tested across three rounds, it has the most beautiful mouthfeel.

**It reads literary first.** Oriana's associations are a knight's beloved in medieval romance and a queen praised in Renaissance song — courtly, bookish, slightly out of time. Its Latin root is felt rather than seen (the way Aesop's Greekness is felt rather than seen). No country comes to mind; if anything comes to mind at all, it is *poetry*.

**The meaning is a gift.** *Oriana* literally means **"the rising golden light"** — dawn-gold. For a lash brand whose promise is beauty that looks given rather than applied, a name that means *the light that arrives naturally* is almost unreasonably apt. And the madrigal lineage adds a second layer that maps to your promise: Oriana was a beauty so admired she was *sung about rather than described* — noticed, not announced, in Renaissance form.

**The lane is clear in Singapore.** Verification found **no Oriana beauty, lash, or salon business in Singapore**. The existing uses are small neighbourhood salons in India and Egypt — geographically distant, unregistered here, and invisible at any scale that matters [3]. Compare the names that died this round: *Ophelia* is blocked by a freshly registered Ophelia Studio Pte. Ltd. in Singapore (ACRA, Nov 2025) plus a 31K-follower local beauty influencer of the same name [4]; *Juno* carries legacy recognition as a Korean hair salon chain that operated in Orchard — the precise country-association we are trying to shed [5]; *Solene* is crowded with three active skincare brands [6].

**One honest trade-off, named openly.** Oriana is four syllables where Aesop is two. It sacrifices a little of the clipped, stamp-like wordmark quality for the softness you asked for — that is the trade, and it is the right one, because your brief ranked sound above compactness. The wordmark compensates with letterspacing (see §7), and the name shortens gracefully in the world's own voice: regulars will call it "Ori" the way Aesop devotees say "the Aesop store," and *Ori* is itself soft, O-led, and ownable as an in-world nickname.

**Lock list, this week, in order:** ACRA name search and reservation ("Oriana Studio" as the registered entity, trading as Oriana); Instagram — target **@oriana.studio.sg** or **@oriana.sg**, with @orianalash as functional fallback; domain — **oriana.sg** preferred, orianastudio.sg fallback; matching TikTok and Lemon8 handles even if dormant. All four before announcing anything — the rule survives every version of this strategy.

### Runners-up, kept on file

If Oriana fails at ACRA or the handles are squatted: **Leto** (LEE-toh — mother of Apollo and Artemis in Greek myth, literally *the one who gave birth to light*; short, soft, O-ended; needs one more verification pass), then **Oona** (OO-nah — the softest sound in the entire exercise, Irish myth-adjacent and Chaplin-literary, held back only because two syllables of pure vowel may feel too slight on a label), then the vetted bench from earlier rounds (Halcyon, Psyche). Blocked this round: Ophelia, Juno, Solene, plus everything blocked in rounds one and two (Selene, Nyx, Eos, Ondine, Sable, Fable, Noctua).

# Part II — The World

## 2. The theme: *The Golden Hour*

The Tomorrowland principle is unchanged from v2 — the world is the brand, the mechanics live inside it — but the world itself turns from dusk to **the golden hour**: that brief, flattering light just after sunrise or before sunset that photographers wait all day for, when everything and everyone looks like the best version of themselves *without a single adjustment being made*. That is not just a prettier metaphor than the evening star — it is a more precise one, because the golden hour is famous for exactly one thing: **making people look naturally beautiful**. Clients don't book a lash appointment; they step into the golden hour and leave carrying it with them.

The one-sentence brand, restated:

> **Oriana is a private lash studio built on the idea of golden-hour light — the hour when everyone looks like themselves, only more so.**

## 3. Positioning, with the Korean angle softened

**For** women in Singapore who want lashes that enhance rather than announce themselves, **Oriana** is the private one-client-at-a-time lash studio **that** designs featherlight, lash-health-first sets people assume are natural — **because** every set is mapped to the individual eye with a quietly rigorous craft standard, in a calm studio that feels like the golden hour, whatever the clock says.

The Korean-technique placement is identical to v2 and unchanged in logic: **craft story, not signage.** The certification lineage lives in the About page and educational content; "Korean-natural" survives as a searchable style descriptor; the SEO layer (Google Business Profile, link-in-bio, hashtags) keeps "Korean lash Singapore" because that remains what the K-Beauty Native segment types into search. Korea as identity is gone. The descriptor lockup:

> **ORIANA · Private Lash Studio**

The three positioning pillars carry over reweighted for the new world: (1) **the world** — the golden-hour ritual, private and unhurried, the anti-salon; (2) **the craft** — mapped-to-your-eye, featherlight, health-first artistry, Korean lineage as supporting evidence; (3) **the proof** — retention shown publicly at week three, honest-refusal policies, the documented craft journey. Audience segments (Natural-First Professional, K-Beauty Native, Salon Refugee), competitive frame, and the *premium-tier brand codes at upper-home-studio prices* posture are unchanged from v1.

## 4. The service menu — named by the light

The menu moves from the arc of an evening to the arc of the light itself, each name plainly translated so no client is ever confused about what she is buying:

| World name | Plain descriptor | Underlying service | Phase 2 price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First Light** | The signature set | Featherlight classic extensions, mapped per eye (Korean-natural styling) | S$78–88 |
| **Full Gold** | The volume set | Soft natural-volume, once certified | S$130–160 |
| **The Lift** | Lash lift + tint | Keratin lift with tint | S$58–68 |
| **The Return** | Infill within 3 weeks | Signature infill | S$45–55 |
| **The Keepsake Kit** | Aftercare at home | Peptide serum + branded cleanser retail | retail margin |

"First Light" survives from v2 because it was always the best name in the system — and under Oriana it gains a second meaning, since the name itself *means* the rising light. It remains the named hero set, the strategic equivalent of J.Lashes' "Tutu" [7]. The system expands without leaving the world (a future bridal package is "The Long Light"; a future training arm is "The Atelier of Hours"). The v1 menu rules stand: no 1D–9D jargon, no falsies on the menu, retailed peptide serum only.

## 5. Brand story — canonical version

> Photographers call it the golden hour — the short stretch of light in which everyone looks like themselves, only more so. Nothing is added. The light simply finds what was already there.
>
> Oriana — an old name from courtly romance, meaning *the rising golden light* — is a private lash studio built on that idea. One client at a time, in quiet, Chesa maps each set to the eye it belongs to: featherlight work in the lash-health-first tradition she trained in, designed to be mistaken for what nature gave you. No salon floor, no packages, no fanfare. Just the hour, the craft, and the compliment that matters most: *"wait — those are yours?"*

The story rules carry over: told in progress-form during Phases 0–1, the certification lineage named specifically once earned, the tracker's craft data (set counts, timings, retention) made public as proof.

## 6. Voice and taglines

The three registers (Editorial / Companion / Care) survive a third version because they were never the variable. The temperature warms from v2's dusk-lit composure to **golden-hour warmth** — unhurried, generous, lit from the side. Vocabulary gains the world's words (*the hour, first light, golden, settle, keep the light*) and keeps the craft words (*map, set, natural lash health, retention*); the banned list is unchanged (*falsies, spidery, mega, promo, cheap*).

Tagline system — primary first, others rotate: **"Noticed, not announced."** (it has survived all three versions because it is the promise) · "Lashes people assume are yours." · "The golden hour, kept." · "The light finds what was already there."

## 7. Visual identity direction

The brief moves from v2's dusk-lit editorial to **golden-hour editorial** — Aesop's restraint, warmed:

| Element | Direction | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Logotype** | ORIANA in a literary serif with generous letterspacing; the long word becomes an asset when tracked wide — it reads as a masthead, not a label. Secondary mark: a small sun-point glyph (·o·) or the single word **Ori** for tight spaces | Letterspacing solves the four-syllable length; "Ori" gives an ownable short form for captions, watermarks, and the eventual product line |
| **Symbol** | A low sun above a horizon line — one stroke, doubling as an open eye with light rising behind the lash line | Same one-stroke discipline as v2; the eye/horizon double read rewards a second look |
| **Palette** | Warm ivory (#F6F0E6) and deep umber-brown ink (#3B2F26) as the primary pair; honey-gold (#C9963F) as the golden-hour accent, used sparingly; soft blush-sand (#E4CDBA) supporting | Placeless, warm, photographs beautifully against skin; deliberately not pink-glitter, not black-and-gold-luxe, not greige-K-minimal, not dusk-blue |
| **Typography** | High-contrast literary serif for headlines; quiet grotesque sans for body and prices | The Aesop code, unchanged: bookish authority, zero decoration |
| **Photography** | True golden-hour warmth as the fixed grade; close-crop eye work on clean skin; before/afters in one fixed template; recurring motifs of hands, tools, window light | The fixed capture setup rule stands — consistency is what makes one artist look like a brand [8] |
| **Materials** | Aftercare card ("Keep the Light" — care instructions styled as a short morning-and-evening rite), appointment card, serum/cleanser labels, linen headrest cover with the sun-point glyph | The aftercare card remains a retention tool and a take-home advertisement [9] |

Execution order remains deliberately unembellished for the early build: Phase 0 needs only the logotype lockup, palette, one Instagram template set, and the aftercare card. Everything else waits for real content in the model era.

# Part III — Experience, Engine, and Activation (carried forward, re-lit)

## 8. The studio ritual → *the golden hour*

The seven-step ritual transfers intact for the third time — confirmation with photo directions, the single signature scent, the mapping conversation, the quiet set, the reveal, the send-off with the rebooking ask ("your Return window is the week of the 14th — Tuesday evening or Saturday morning?"), and the 48-hour check-in. Under the new world the room is lit warm and low-angled like late afternoon regardless of the clock, the phone basket is "leaving the day at the door," and the reveal mirror carries one engraved line — *noticed, not announced*. **The Cavapoo recommendation stands**: dog-friendly as an advertised brand feature, a guaranteed dog-free option, and a dog-free lash room during treatments — the puppy is the studio's resident golden retriever joke waiting to happen (a golden-hour companion who is not, in fact, golden) [10]. Trust infrastructure is unchanged: HDB registration noted quietly, insurance stated, visible hygiene protocol, patch tests, honest-refusal policy.

## 9. The content and lead engine

Channels, cadence, funnel phases, referral mechanics — all carry over unchanged: Instagram primary, WhatsApp as the booking rail, Lemon8 and Google Business Profile supporting, press pitches in Phase 2 [7] [11]. The four pillars, re-lit:

| Pillar | Share | Re-themed content |
|---|---|---|
| **The Work** | 40% | Before/afters in the fixed template; week-three retention posts captioned *"three weeks in, still golden"* — proof remains the killer differentiator |
| **The Craft Journey** | 25% | The 100-set counter becomes *"100 sets before the golden hour opens"* — a countdown whose finale is the day real pricing launches |
| **Lash Intelligence** | 20% | Health-first education, styling explainers (Korean-natural as a style among styles, explained by someone trained in it), aftercare myths |
| **The World** | 15% | Window light in the studio, the scent, the ritual details, the dog, Chesa herself, and the Oriana story — the madrigals, the meaning of the name — told in fragments |

## 10. Activation — the first 90 days

The calendar logic holds (travel until 25 July; certification from early August; brand work sized around 8–10 weekly craft hours). Weeks 1–2: lock the Oriana name across ACRA, handles, and domain; finalise the dog-policy language. Weeks 3–4: produce the minimum identity kit in the golden palette plus the fixed photo corner — ideally positioned to catch real late-afternoon window light, which becomes the brand's actual production asset. Weeks 5–6: launch Instagram with the pinned name-story post ("Oriana — the rising golden light") and the counter announcement. Weeks 7–10: certification-era content rhythm. Weeks 11–13: model-era machine — WhatsApp flows, consent note, ritual checklist, first documented model sets. Beyond day 90, nothing brand-related jumps a phase gate the tracker hasn't confirmed.

## 11. Measurement

Identical across all three versions, because the numbers were never the variable: profile-to-WhatsApp conversion, saves and shares per post, referral share of new clients (>40% by mid-Phase 2), Google reviews (25+ at 4.9 by end of Phase 2). Follower count remains explicitly not a KPI.

## A closing note from your co-founder

Three rounds of naming is not indecision — it is exactly how good names get found. Each constraint you added (placeless, then literary-mythological, then soft with the O) cut the field harder, and the survivor is stronger for it: a name that sounds like what the studio does. What I would do now is stress-test it in the real world for 48 hours before locking: say "Welcome to Oriana" out loud, text a friend "I'm opening a studio called Oriana" and watch what they ask, and check how it feels signing off a WhatsApp booking with it. If it still feels right on Friday, lock the four registrations that day and we never look back. The world is ready the moment the name is.
